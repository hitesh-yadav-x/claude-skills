## 2026-11-10 · equivalent-fractions
- The idea: Two fractions can look different and still be the same amount. If you cut every piece into smaller pieces, you get more pieces, but each one is smaller, so the amount stays the same. 1/2 is the same as 2/4.
- How it shows up: You are asked whether two fractions are equal, or to fill in a missing number, like 1/2 = ?/4. The trap is thinking more pieces means more to eat.
- Watch for: Saying two fractions are equal because you remember it. Say why: what was each piece cut into?

## 2026-11-13 · adding-fractions
- The idea: The bottom number is the size of the pieces. You can only add pieces that are the same size, so first cut both fractions into pieces of a size they share. Then add the top numbers.
- How it shows up: Two fractions with different bottom numbers and a plus sign, often inside a story about pizza or a recipe. The trap is adding the tops and adding the bottoms.
- Watch for: Adding the bottom numbers. Check your answer: adding can't make it smaller than a part you started with.
- Note: Halves and thirds can both be cut into sixths, so sixths is the size they share.
